How to choose a rendering:
1. The decisions above come first.
2. Then the established form in {{target_language}}, as the references use it. A person, place, ship or event that already has a name in {{target_language}} keeps it; do not transliterate afresh. Where the references disagree, take the form used for the article's own subject, and mark the entry (check).
3. Then the tradition of the field in {{target_language}}: the words its own writers and practitioners use.
4. Only then a careful rendering of your own, marked (check).

List, using the book's own spellings as the keys:
- False friends and old senses: words a translator working from the modern language would get wrong, with the sense they have in this book. These matter most, so work through the text looking for them. Say what each word is NOT as well as what it is.
- People: one fixed form each, including the variants the book uses for the same person. Note any grammatical gender or declension a translator could get wrong.
- Places, ships, animals, institutions.
- Recurring technical terms of the book's field, and units with what they really measure here.
- Abbreviations.

Leave out ordinary words with no trap in them; a long glossary gets followed less closely. Do not include material that is not in the book. Write the notes in English, and the renderings in {{target_language}}.

Output only the glossary, in Markdown, with one "## " heading per group and one line per entry.
